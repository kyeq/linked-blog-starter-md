### $\forall$ (for all) and $\exists$ (there exists)
**for all** and **there exists** are quantifiers.
they take a predicate with a gap, like P(x), and turn it into a full statement that's either true / false.

### What is a predicate?
A predicate is a **property of a relationship that a thing can have or not have.** It's a sentence with a blank in it, and the blank gets filled in with an object.

**Example**
For example,
> "____ is a prime number"

With nothing in the blank, it isn't true or false yet.
That sentence with the blank is the predicate. We name it and use x for the blank:
>**Prime(x) = "x is a prime number*

A predicate is basically a method that takes something and returns a boolean.

### Examples with $\forall$ and $\exists$
**"All students are tired"**
*For every x, if x is a student, then x is tired.*
$\forall x (S(x) \to T)$

**"Some students are tired"**
*There is an x such that x is a student and x is tired.*
$\exists x (S(x) \land T(x))$
